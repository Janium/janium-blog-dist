Every institution with a collection reaches the same bottleneck sooner or later: the material exists, but to find it and use it, it has to be described. A book, a thesis, a case file, a photograph, a recording, a contract: each one needs a catalog record in a standard format, produced by someone who knows how to catalog. Meanwhile the material piles up, often already digitized but undescribed.

Janium Collect is the service with which Janium catalogs that material using artificial intelligence. The institution does not buy or install a program: it hires the cataloging. Janium receives the documents where the institution already keeps them, catalogs them with Collect, and delivers the records in the format and the catalog the institution uses. Whoever catalogs stops typing every record from scratch and moves to reviewing the ones they receive.

What you hire

You hire the result: catalog records for a collection or for a steady flow of documents. The service is quoted according to the collection: the type of material, the volume, the description format, and the level of review the institution needs.

Before starting, the Janium team defines with the institution how each collection is described: the format, the cataloging profile, the fields its catalog expects, and the vocabularies it uses. That configuration is what makes the records come out the way the institution itself would produce them, and it is refined with the first batches, which the institution reviews before loading into the catalog becomes automatic.

What it delivers

Each record comes out in the institution’s format:

  • MARC21 and UNIMARC for library catalogs.
  • ISAD-G for archives, with the hierarchy of fonds, section, and series.
  • Dublin Core for repositories and simple descriptions.
  • CDWA for works of art and cultural objects, with the Getty vocabulary.

The records arrive with a quality report, which gives a score and the problems detected in each record, and with the provenance of each value: whether it was transcribed from the document, deduced or written by the AI, translated, or calculated by a rule. The records are delivered to the catalog of the institution, whether Janium or another, in a batch ready to load.

What material it starts from

The material arrives through the channels the institution already uses: a folder in the cloud or on its server, email, a spreadsheet, or the Janium capture app, with photos taken in front of the item. Collect catalogs:

  • books, theses, journals, and documents in PDF, born digital or scanned;
  • photographs of covers, copyright pages, and indexes;
  • lists and inventories in spreadsheets;
  • photographs, artworks, and objects from the image and its catalog card;
  • audio and video, from the recording.

There are articles by type of collection: libraries, periodicals collections, photo archives, audiovisual collections, artistic heritage, government archives, corporate documentation, and numismatics.

How it is reviewed

The person who catalogs stays at the center, and what changes is where they put their attention. The records arrive in the institution’s catalog, where they are reviewed and corrected like any other record. The quality report says where to start: a record with a low score arrives with a note asking for review, and an incomplete or altered batch is not loaded.

A library with catalogers can review record by record. An archive or a firm that describes thousands of documents reviews by exception, starting with what the report flags. The provenance of each value says what is worth checking: what was transcribed from the document needs less attention than what the AI deduced.

Where it is processed

Collect processes in the cloud with a commercial AI provider. When the documents cannot leave the institution, the processing can run on its own infrastructure, with or without an anonymized query to the cloud; the options are covered in data sovereignty.

The principle that underpins trust

Collect does not just transcribe: it identifies the material and completes the record with values the source did not carry, such as subjects or classification. That enrichment is the value, and also the risk: a value that sounds reasonable but is wrong can stay in the catalog for years looking correct.

That is why each value carries its provenance. What can be verified is verified: names and subjects can be checked against the authority files and vocabularies the institution uses, and they come out with their source noted. What has a rule, such as the Cutter number of the call number, is calculated by rules, not by the AI. And what the AI contributes is marked as inferred or generated, never unmarked. The reasoning is in trusting a catalog made with AI.

What it does not do

Collect enriches, but it does not fabricate: a value the document does not carry and that has nothing to be deduced from, such as a measurement nobody took, is not filled in with a guess. It does not organize the archive for the institution: the hierarchy comes from the inventory, the folders, or the sections the institution defines. And it does not replace the judgment of whoever knows the collection: the quality of the records depends on that of the source material and on the review the institution decides to do.

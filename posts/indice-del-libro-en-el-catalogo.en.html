Janium Collect can publish a book’s table of contents as a document of its record in the Janium catalog, starting from the photos taken while cataloging it. Anyone looking up the catalog opens the table of contents from the catalog card and sees the chapters before requesting the book, and the library gets this without scanning the volume: it is enough for the capture to tell which photo is the cover, which is the copyright page and which are the table of contents.

Photos are requested by function

The Janium capture app can ask for a book’s photos in separate fields: the cover, the copyright page and the table of contents. In the same form, the person holding the book enters the page count and the dimensions, two values that are better measured with the volume in hand than from a photo.

Collect joins the photos into one document in that order and reads the item the way a person turning the pages would. The page count and the dimensions go to the physical description (300), and the record number is taken from the form. Because Collect knows which pages each field supplied, it can handle the table of contents pages separately from the rest.

The table of contents as a document of the record

When the institution turns it on, the batch of records includes, next to each record, a document with only the table of contents photos. The photos are reduced to 1,600 pixels per side, so a table of contents of five photos comes to about 900 KB, and the load script links it in Janium to the same digital object as the cover thumbnail, with the access level the institution sets. If a record is loaded again, its object is updated.

A book without a table of contents goes out without a document, and the record is loaded all the same. The institution chooses with a parameter what to publish: only the table of contents, another combination of fields, such as the copyright page plus the table of contents, or no document at all.

If the configured field is not one of those that arrive from the app, the batch is held and the error is recorded, so that it does not go out without any table of contents.

The first batches are reviewed before loading

While an institution reviews its first records, the complete batches, with the flat file (FLAT), the quality report, the cover thumbnails, the table of contents documents and the load script, are assembled in a folder on the Collect server that the catalog loader cannot reach. The team loads them by hand and checks in the catalog how the catalog card and its table of contents look, and once the institution approves the result, the following batches move to automatic loading. Delivery to the catalog is covered in Integrating AI cataloging with your catalog (ILS).

Photo resolution matters in the record

A photo taken with the built-in camera of the web form arrives at about 430 × 570 pixels, while a photo uploaded from the phone’s camera is several thousand pixels per side. In a test with 350 books from a university library, the average record score was 92.6 with the small photos and 95.2 with the phone photos, and the four records below 70 came from small photos. That is why the recommendation to whoever captures is to upload the phone photo instead of shooting inside the form.

Limits of the table of contents on the catalog card

The photo. The document shows the table of contents photos as they were taken, reduced. A blurry or small shot looks just as bad on the catalog card as on the phone.

The year. When the book does not print the year, or the photo does not let it be read, the call number comes out without a year. In the 350-book test this happened in 51, most of them textbooks that do not print the year or low-resolution photos.

Capture by fields. The table of contents can be separated only if the app asks for the photos by function. A submission with all the photos together produces the record, but Collect does not know which ones are the table of contents and does not publish the document.

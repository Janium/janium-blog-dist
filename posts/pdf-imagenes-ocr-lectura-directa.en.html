Before cataloging a PDF, Janium Collect transcribes the content of its pages, the work OCR software used to do, and catalogs from that transcription. The step is the same for a PDF exported from a word processor, for the scan of a thirty-year-old book, and for photographs of a case file, so a collection that mixes all three goes through a single path and the files do not need to be sorted before uploading.

Transcribe first, catalog afterward

Transcribing a page and deciding what each value is are two different jobs, and Collect does them separately. The transcription keeps the page order, the tables, the accents, and the original language, without translating or interpreting, and the record is built afterward with the fields of the format the institution uses.

The practical reason lies in dates and numbers: with the transcription at hand, a day and month that were swapped or a volume number that was misread can be checked against what the page says. That same check feeds the provenance marking, which distinguishes in the record the data that is written in the document from the data the language model filled in.

How much of the document is read

When the document is complete as a PDF, Collect can read all of it, up to five hundred pages, and it is worth letting it do so. With the whole thesis at hand, the summary comes from the entire work and not only from the introduction, the subjects reflect what the thesis covers from beginning to end, and the access points include the people, places, and institutions that appear in the chapters and not only on the title page. Each institution sets, per collection, how many pages are read.

Reading only the first pages is the technique for when the document is not fully digitized and there are no plans to digitize it. In a historical archive, or in a library that photographs the title page, the copyright page, and the table of contents of each book, those few pages are enough for a workable record: title, contributors, edition, publication, and, with the table of contents, an accurate idea of the content. The record produced this way is useful, but its subjects and summary rest on what those pages say, and whoever reviews it knows that.

The extent of the document, the total number of pages that goes into the physical description, comes from the complete file and not from the pages that were read.

Photos from the capture app and loose images

Photos taken with the item in hand in the Janium capture app are joined into a single document and follow the same path as a PDF, as do those that arrive grouped by item through the other input channels. A loose photograph or plan, where there is no text to transcribe, is cataloged from the image, and its data is marked as inferred.

Limits

  • Quality of the original. A scan with faded ink, stains, or old typography is transcribed less well than a clear page, and whatever is misread enters the record as transcribed, where the review finds it.
  • Handwriting, multiple columns, and non-Latin scripts. They are transcribed through the same path, without specific handling for each case.

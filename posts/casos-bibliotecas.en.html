In a library, the purchased book, the rare book, the ebook and the thesis end up in the same catalog under the same standard, but each reaches Janium Collect from different sources, and that decides which values in the record are transcribed, which ones the model completes, which ones a rule calculates and which ones are left to whoever has the book in hand. Almost the whole record of a purchased book comes from the vendor’s list, whereas for a rare book Collect produces the descriptive base and the copy is described with the book in hand. Every record comes out with the provenance of each value marked, and with that the library knows where to concentrate the review for each material.

The general collection

Purchased books arrive by three routes. The vendor’s list, with author, title, ISBN and year, is turned into records without retyping it, as explained in Cataloging from lists and spreadsheets. When there is no list, whoever has the book in hand photographs the cover and the legal page with the Janium capture app, and if the book is already digitized, the record comes from the PDF.

In all three cases, what the source contains is transcribed and what the model completes, such as the subjects or the summary, is marked as added. The model proposes the classification number, whether Dewey, LC or UDC, while the Cutter is calculated with the table that matches the standard, Cutter-Sanborn or the LC one, as explained in The Cutter number and the ISAD-G level. The full call number goes in the 852, or in the 995 if the record is UNIMARC.

The rare book collection

A rare book is not described only by its work, because the specific copy matters: a bookplate, the stamp of a former library, a donation note or the binding. Collect reads what appears in the images, which are the cover, the colophon and the pages with marks, and produces the descriptive base of the record.

For a heritage collection, the instruction in that collection’s profile asks to gather the bookplate, the stamps and the donation in a provenance note (561), and to leave the imprint as it appears, without completing it with a guess. The binding and the state of conservation stay outside the record that Collect proposes, because that description is made with the book in hand. In this collection, the review by someone who knows the holdings is part of the method.

Ebooks

Collect accepts EPUB files: it converts them to text and reads them the same way as a digital PDF, with the cover, the credits page and the front matter. The metadata that the file itself contains is not carried over field by field into the record; the model reads what the book shows on its pages, and what it adds is marked as in any other record.

The commercial subjects and the publisher’s name forms do not always match those of a catalog. The subjects the model proposes arrive marked, and the decision about the headings remains with the cataloger.

Theses

A thesis has a fairly regular title page: the institution, the program, the title, the author, the advisor and the year. With the thesis profile in MARC21, the record carries the thesis note (502) with the degree, the institution and the year, the advisor in a 700 with their role and the faculty or program in a 710. What the title page does not state comes out empty and is left for review.

At many universities the thesis goes to the institutional repository and not to the catalog, or to both places. The library decides the destination, and when it wants the work in both places two records come out, each in its own schema. That decision, and the Dublin Core record for the repository, are covered in Records of a university library.

The library sets the standard, the file and the destination

Each institution declares in its configuration the format it catalogs in, and without that declaration the record comes out in MARC21. A MARC21 record follows RDA: it carries the standard in the 040 and the content, media and carrier types in fields 336, 337 and 338. The UNIMARC record is generated directly in that format, without going through a conversion from MARC21, and the difference between the two formats is explained in How to generate MARC21 and UNIMARC records with AI.

The batch format is also chosen: flat file (FLAT), ISO 2709 or MarcEdit text, depending on what the catalog that will load it accepts. Collect delivers the batch and the library loads it by the means it already has, as detailed in Integrating AI cataloging with your catalog (ILS).

The library does not choose the format record by record: it sets it for each input channel, such as a folder, so that what comes in through the thesis folder comes out with the schema and destination of theses, and what comes in through the purchases folder comes out as a catalog record. The channels are described in Where material enters Collect: folders, cloud, email and the capture app.

Where review concentrates

Each record comes out with a provenance file that says, field by field, whether the value was transcribed from the source, inferred or generated by the model, calculated by a rule or translated by the system, and the record also carries a summary of that provenance in a note (588 in MARC21). Each record also has a quality score from 0 to 100, and those with the lowest scores carry a review note.

Review is distributed unevenly. In the general collection with a vendor list, a spot check usually suffices, in ebooks and theses the subjects and whatever the title page did not state are examined, and in the rare book collection the review is substantial.

Limits in a library

Classification. The model proposes the classification number and it is reviewed like any other added value. What a rule calculates is the Cutter.

The rare copy. The binding, the state of conservation and missing pages stay outside the record that Collect proposes and are described with the book in hand.

Ebook metadata. Collect reads the content of the EPUB and sets aside the record the publisher embedded in the file, so a value that appears only in that record does not reach the catalog record.

The advisor’s role. In MARC21 the thesis advisor’s role goes as text in the 700. In UNIMARC, the table of relator codes that Collect uses has no code for thesis advisor.
